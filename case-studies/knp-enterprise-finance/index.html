The operating environment

A sale can involve birds, kilograms, credit, and cash.

KNP purchases poultry from suppliers and sells to shops, hotels, and individual customers. Live-bird trading and dressed-product sales share the same business, but do not consume inventory in the same way. Products can be purchased under one source category and sold under several names.

A party can be both a customer and a supplier. Counter staff need to issue bills while the owner needs traceable balances and a view of the day's activity. The application connects these tasks rather than treating billing, stock, and finance as separate records.

The challenge behind the screens

Credit sales must reach the right account, payments must update the correct balance, and birds sent for dressing must not be deducted twice. Physical closing counts must also carry into the next day's opening.

01 / Counter billing

Handle regular and dressed products on the same bill.

Configurable item shortcuts support repetitive counter entry. Bill lines capture bird count, weight, rate, and amount, with automatic weight calculation where required. Sales can be recorded on credit, partly paid, or fully paid.

Daily numbering, searchable history, bill references, party suggestions, stored phone details, and authorised editing support both service at the counter and later checks. Safeguards address numbering conflicts and duplicate submissions when owner and staff work at the same outlet.

02 / Purchasing and party accounts

Follow a balance back to its transactions.

Purchases, sales, and payments connect to party records through direct entry or structured file imports. Accounts include opening balances, running histories, bill references, payment direction and method, and search by name and date range.

Receivables and payables are calculated separately, including for parties that act as both customer and supplier.

03 / Poultry inventory

Expected stock and counted stock answer different questions.

Inventory tracks bird count and weight. Source bird types are distinguished from selling names, so retail products and shortcuts can be mapped to the stock they consume.

The live-stock reconciliation rule

Expected live closing stock = Opening live stock + Purchases − Live sales − Birds taken for dressing − Mortality.

Live sales, dressing, and mortality are separate movements. Birds taken for dressing leave live inventory at that step, rather than being deducted again when dressed products are sold.

Carry the physical count forward.

Staff enter actual physical closing stock and compare it with the expected figure. The previous day's actual closing stock becomes the next day's opening.

Expected stock reflects the transaction records; actual stock reflects what staff counted. Keeping both makes discrepancies visible instead of treating the calculation as a physical count.

04 / Daily sheets and reporting

Review the day, then share the relevant records.

Daily sheets bring together sales, purchases, collections, party balances, and stock for a selected date. Statements support account follow-up, while stock summaries show differences between expected and counted inventory.

Excel, PDF, and report-image exports serve different reporting needs. WhatsApp sharing workflows help staff prepare bills and statements for customers. Missing source information or unreliable calculations are shown with warning or unavailable states.

05 / Data onboarding and access

Check imported records before using them.

Setup covers party directories, opening financial balances, and opening stock. Daily imports support supplier purchases, customer sales, and payments.

Downloadable templates, upload previews, and row-level errors help users prepare and check structured files. Owner and staff access separate responsibilities, including permission to edit existing bills.

06 / Access and thermal printing

Connect browser billing to the receipt printer.

Responsive layouts support laptop and mobile use, with an installable web-app experience. A local print bridge connects browser billing to thermal receipt printing at the counter.

The implementation included Windows startup setup to reduce repeated manual printer-service steps. Receipt layouts were refined to align bird-count, weight, and amount totals for counter use.

Software delivery includes the working environment.

The scope extended beyond application screens to local hardware integration and the steps staff take to start their day.

Production refinement

Improve daily use without losing financial continuity.

Client feedback informed navigation, task grouping, form labels, and scrolling. Growing bill history was addressed through paginated loading and more focused data retrieval.

When the ledger model changed, a dated transition preserved historical calculations and carried balances into the upgraded model.

Reported technical validation

205 parties across three outlets.

The project's final recorded reconciliation reported no balance-preservation discrepancies across 205 parties and three outlets. This checks balance continuity during the ledger transition; it is not an independent financial audit or a measure of commercial performance.
